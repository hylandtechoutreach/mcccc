# Quantum Computing
This session introduces Quantum Computing at a _very_ high level. By the end, students will run their code on a real quantum computer hopefully!

|-|-|
| Warm-Up | 15m |
| Lecture | 15m |

| Blooket | 10m |

## Warm-Up
Have the students line up. Each student will be assigned a team based on a coin flip - they will join either Team Heads or Team Tails. Keep track of which students join which team. Members of the winning team will receive a ticket.

In quantum computing, qubits can exist in _superposition_, sort of like a coin spinning in the air - it's only when measured that the state of a qubit collapses into _information_ in the classic sense. A coin is either heads or tails, but a spinning coin is kind of both at once.

Another aspect of quantum computing is that it could break RSA encryption. Students will have to decrypt their team's encrypted messages to see what to do next! There should be a couple copies of the [worksheets](./Assets/WarmUpEncryptedMessages.pdf) printed (sets of seven pages for each team).

### Process
Once students have been split into teams, they should work together to decrypt the messages. There are seven total pages, so they should be encouraged to split into seven groups and each work on a page. They are NOT allowed to use the computers EXCEPT for actually going to the URL and entering the password (e.g., no auto decrypting using an online tool). They can have extra paper to work out solutions if needed.

Once a team sees the "CORRECT" screen, they win! Only one team should be able to claim the victory (the other team should see a message saying "you were too late" even if they had the correct password).
